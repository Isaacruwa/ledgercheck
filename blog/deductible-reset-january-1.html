Does Your Deductible Reset on January 1? What to Check Before Year-End

EOBCheck · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer It depends on your plan. If your plan runs on a calendar year, your deductible resets on January 1. If it runs on a plan year that renews in another month, it resets on that renewal date instead. Your insurer's member portal shows the exact dates, and a few plans also credit deductible amounts you paid late in the year toward the next one.

Calendar year vs. plan year

A deductible is the amount you pay for covered care before your plan starts paying its share. How long that count lasts depends on how your plan is set up:

  • Calendar-year plans run from January 1 to December 31. Your deductible and your out-of-pocket maximum both reset to $0 on January 1.
  • Plan-year plans run for 12 months from the date your coverage renews, such as July 1 or October 1. Your deductible resets on that renewal date, not on January 1.

Some employers renew their plan in one month but count the deductible on a calendar year, which is why the dates on your plan documents are the ones to trust.

How to find your reset date

  1. Check your insurer's member portal. Most portals show a deductible progress bar with a date range next to it. A range of January 1 through December 31 means a calendar-year deductible. Any other 12-month range means it resets on the first day of that period.
  2. Read your summary of benefits. It lists your deductible, your out-of-pocket maximum and the plan year.
  3. Call member services or your employer's HR team. Ask: "When does my deductible reset, and does my plan have a fourth-quarter carryover?"

Does anything carry over?

Usually not. But some plans offer a fourth-quarter carryover: deductible amounts you pay in the last three months of the plan year count toward that year and the next one. Plans that are paired with a health savings account often do not offer it. The only way to know is to check your plan documents or ask your insurer.

If you join a plan partway through the year, the deductible is generally not prorated. The full amount applies until the plan year ends, even if only a few months are left.

What the reset looks like on an EOB

The date of service decides which plan year a claim counts toward. So the same visit can leave you with a very different patient responsibility depending on when it happens. Here is an example with made-up numbers: a $1,000 deductible, 20% coinsurance after it and a $250 allowed amount.

That is why a bill that looks too high in January is not always a mistake. The new year's deductible may be taking the first claims. Check the applied to deductible line on the EOB before you assume something is wrong. If the numbers still do not add up, our guide on why a bill and EOB do not match covers the usual causes.

A year-end checklist

  • Find your reset date using the steps above, so you know whether January 1 applies to you.
  • Look at how much of your deductible and out-of-pocket maximum you have used. Your portal or your latest EOBs will show it.
  • Ask about a fourth-quarter carryover before you decide when to schedule non-urgent care. Do not delay care you need for your health because of a deductible, and talk to your doctor about timing.
  • Keep your EOBs from this year. You will want them to check January bills against the right plan year.
  • Check each bill against its EOB as it arrives, especially for visits close to the year-end or the renewal date.

This is general information, not insurance, legal or financial advice. Plan rules vary, so confirm the details with your insurer or employer.

FAQ

Does my deductible reset on January 1?
Only if your plan runs on a calendar year. Plans on a different plan year reset on their renewal date instead.

How do I find out when my deductible resets?
Check the dates beside your deductible progress in your insurer's portal, your summary of benefits, or ask member services or HR.

Does my deductible carry over?
Usually not. Some plans offer a fourth-quarter carryover, but plans paired with a health savings account often do not.

Is my deductible smaller if I join mid-year?
No. An annual deductible is generally not prorated for people who enroll partway through the year.
