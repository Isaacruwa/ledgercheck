How to Ask for an Itemized Medical Bill

EOBCheck · September 2, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer Call the provider's billing department (or use their patient portal) and ask for an "itemized statement" instead of a summary bill. It's a routine request — you don't need a special reason, and providers handle these regularly.

Why a summary bill isn't enough

A summary bill just shows one total. An itemized bill breaks that total into every individual charge — each test, supply, procedure code, and fee listed separately. Duplicate charges, coding errors, and services you never actually received are far easier to spot line by line than buried in a single number.

How to actually request one

  1. Find the billing department's phone number on your bill, or check for a patient portal message option
  2. Ask specifically for an "itemized statement" or "itemized bill" — this phrase gets you the right document faster than just asking for "details"
  3. If calling, note the date, who you spoke with, and confirm how it will be sent (mail, email, or portal)
  4. If you'd rather have a written record, email or use the portal's messaging feature instead of just calling
"Hi, I'd like to request an itemized statement for my account, showing each individual charge rather than just the total. Could you send that to me?"

What to do once you have it

Scan for anything that appears twice, any service you don't recognize, and any charge with a date that doesn't match when you were actually seen. Then compare the itemized total against your EOB's patient responsibility figure — a mismatch there is exactly what's worth raising with the provider in writing.
