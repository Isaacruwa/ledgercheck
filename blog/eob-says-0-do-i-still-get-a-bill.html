EOB Says $0 — Do I Still Get a Bill?

EOBCheck · September 2, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer No, generally not. If your EOB shows $0 as your patient responsibility, your provider shouldn't send you a bill asking for payment on that same claim. If one arrives anyway, that's a mismatch worth questioning directly — not something to pay without checking first.

Why an EOB shows $0 in the first place

A few completely normal reasons this happens:

  • The service was fully covered. Many preventive care visits (annual checkups, certain screenings) are covered at 100% with no patient cost-sharing under most plans.
  • You'd already met your deductible or out-of-pocket maximum for the plan year, so the plan covered the full allowed amount.
  • The claim was adjusted in your favor after the insurer reviewed it — sometimes an initial estimate gets corrected downward.

So why would a bill still show up?

A few possibilities, roughly in order of how common they are:

  • The provider billed before receiving the finalized EOB, using an outdated estimate
  • The bill reflects a different claim or date of service than the $0 EOB
  • A billing error — the patient responsibility field was entered incorrectly
  • The provider is attempting to balance bill for an amount your insurer already covered

What to actually do

Compare the date of service and provider name on both documents first — make sure you're not comparing a bill to an EOB for a completely different visit. If they match and the bill still asks for payment, request a written explanation of the specific amount before paying anything.
