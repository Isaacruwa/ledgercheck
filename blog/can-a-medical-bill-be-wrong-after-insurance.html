Can a Medical Bill Be Wrong After Insurance Pays?

EOBCheck · September 2, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer Yes. Insurance paying its share doesn't guarantee the remaining bill is accurate. Your insurer checks claims against coverage rules and negotiated rates — not necessarily against every line item's accuracy. Billing errors can survive that process completely untouched.

Why "insurance already paid" doesn't mean "bill is correct"

It's an easy assumption: if insurance reviewed and paid the claim, the process must have caught any mistakes. In practice, insurers are checking whether a service is covered and applying the negotiated allowed amount — they're not auditing whether the provider billed the same service twice, or used the wrong billing code, or made a data-entry error on your balance.

What can still be wrong even after insurance processes the claim

  • Duplicate charges — the same service or supply listed more than once
  • Coding errors — a service billed under a different, more expensive code than what actually happened
  • Balance billing — being charged more than your EOB says you owe
  • Simple data entry mistakes — a manually typed balance that doesn't match the EOB math
  • Timing issues — the bill printed before the insurer's final numbers came through

How to actually check

Compare your bill's "amount you owe" directly against your EOB's "patient responsibility" figure for the same date of service. If they match, you're set. If they don't, request an itemized statement before paying — it's the fastest way to spot a duplicate or miscoded charge.
