FSA and HSA Year-End Deadline: What to Do Before December 31

EOBCheck · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer Health FSA money is often use-it-or-lose-it, so unspent funds can disappear at the end of your plan year unless your employer offers a grace period or a limited carryover. HSA money never expires and rolls over every year. Check your plan's exact deadline now, and keep your EOBs, since they are usually the proof you need to claim an expense.

Grace period vs. carryover

If your employer offers one of these, your FSA money does not vanish on the last day of the plan year:

  • Grace period: extra time, up to two and a half months, to spend the old year's money.
  • Carryover: a limited amount of unused money moves into the next plan year. For 2026 the most a plan can allow is $680.

A plan can offer only one, and some offer neither. There is also usually a separate claims deadline, called a run-out period, for turning in receipts for the old year. It is easy to miss, so look for it in your plan documents.

Where your EOB fits in

Your out-of-pocket costs, such as copays, deductible amounts and coinsurance, are generally the kind of expense an FSA or HSA can cover. Your EOB is what shows them:

  • It is often accepted as proof. An EOB lists the date of service, the provider and your patient responsibility, which many FSA administrators ask for when you submit a claim. Check what yours accepts.
  • It tells you what you really owe. Compare the bill to the EOB before you pay or claim, so you do not spend tax-advantaged money on an amount that is wrong. Our guide on what to do before you pay a medical bill shows how.
  • The date of service usually decides the year. Care generally counts toward the plan year in which you received it, even if the bill arrives or is paid later. Check your plan for how it treats unpaid bills at year-end.

A year-end checklist

  1. Check your FSA balance and your plan year end. Your benefits portal or your FSA administrator shows both.
  2. Find out whether you have a grace period or a carryover, and when the claims deadline is.
  3. Gather this year's EOBs and bills for copays, deductible amounts and other eligible costs you have not claimed yet.
  4. Check each bill against its EOB before you submit it.
  5. Think about care you already need, like prescriptions, dental work or glasses, and use any remaining FSA money on things you were going to buy anyway. Do not buy things you do not need just to use up the balance.
  6. If you have an HSA, there is no spending deadline. You can still plan your contributions for the year.

If your plan's deductible resets on January 1, our guide on whether your deductible resets on January 1 explains how that changes what you owe in the new year.

FAQ

Does FSA money expire on December 31?
Often, yes. A health FSA is generally use-it-or-lose-it. Some plans give a grace period or a limited carryover, so check your plan documents for the exact deadline.

What is the difference between a grace period and a carryover?
A grace period gives you up to two and a half extra months to spend old money. A carryover moves a limited amount into the next year. For 2026 the carryover maximum is $680. A plan can offer only one of them.

Does HSA money expire?
No. It rolls over every year and belongs to you.

Can I use my EOB to claim an expense?
Often, yes. It shows the date, provider and your patient responsibility, which many administrators ask for. Rules vary, so check yours.
